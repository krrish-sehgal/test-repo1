# Where to look for news

The posting routine and any research for posts use this list. Search across it, not just one outlet. Every post's notes must name the sources actually used, and say which ones were blocked or paywalled.

## How to treat a source

- **Primary beats secondary.** A company's press release, blog post, filing or a CEO's own post is the primary source for what the company claims. News coverage is how you confirm it and find the angle.
- **A press release is still the company's claim.** Valuations, user counts and "fastest ever" come from the company. Say so in the post if it matters ("the company says").
- **Two independent outlets for any fact that isn't the company's own claim.** If only one outlet has it, drop it or label it.
- **Watch for competitors and sellers.** A recap written by a rival product, or an "article" that ends by selling a tool, is not neutral. Flag it, and confirm with the project's own page. (Lesson from 2026-09-28: a competitor's recap of the Grok 72-hour build missed that the team pivoted to a game.)
- **Blocked pages.** businesswire.com, Bloomberg, FT, CNBC and some X pages block direct fetches. Use a mirror (below) or several search-result summaries that agree.

## Funding and company announcements

Funding rounds count as a story when at least one of these holds:
- the round is $100M or more, or an Indian round is $20M or more
- the valuation jumped 2x or more within about six months
- the founders or the product are already known to the user's audience
- there is a surprising human detail (Instinct went from $2.5B to $10B in one month without sharing a single user number)

Tell it as a story, never as "X raises $Y".

## AI and tech news

- Latent Space's AINews digest (a daily summary of AI posts on X, Reddit and Discord)
- The Verge, Ars Technica, The Register, Wired
- Reuters, Bloomberg, FT, CNBC (often paywalled or blocked; confirm through other outlets)
- MarkTechPost, Unite.AI (fast, sometimes thin; confirm important claims elsewhere)
