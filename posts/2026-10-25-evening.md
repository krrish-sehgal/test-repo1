what the story is: the LiteLLM 1.107.0 supply chain compromise, as laid out in LiteLLM's own 23 Oct root cause report plus Socket's timeline research. A config file carrying the master key for LiteLLM's experimental Agent Platform was pushed to the public LiteLLM-Labs/lite-harness repo on 27 May 2026; the commit was force-pushed over but the key was never revoked — 27 May to 10 Oct is the 136 days. On 10 Oct the attacker used the key to reach the Agent Platform and extracted a GitHub CLI OAuth token tied to an organisation owner with no scope restrictions, then committed malicious workflows (first at 22:11:45 UTC on 10 Oct). Socket found those secret-exfiltration workflows ran 11 days and took ~20 org secrets. On 21 Oct a maintainer-token push was blocked by branch protection at 10:08:29 UTC and an owner-token push succeeded at 10:08:34 after protections were disabled — that five-second gap is in the timeline, not inferred. 1.107.0 went to PyPI, Docker Hub and GHCR; PyPI served it ~11:39–13:23 UTC (about an hour 45) and container tags stayed poisoned until ~14:20 UTC. The payload ran at Python startup without the app importing LiteLLM, and took SSH files, git and registry tokens and environment variables to a lookalike domain registered 13 Oct. 92M+ monthly downloads is Socket's figure. The closing beat is from LiteLLM's own report: a relaxed release policy allowed any two engineers to trigger and approve a release, and the actor used one stolen token to trigger and another to approve. Note for future runs: an earlier secondary write-up phrased this as "two engineers approve their own release", which a search flagged as unsupported; the primary report does support the one-token-triggers, one-token-approves version used here, so it stayed in. Deliberately left out: Wiz's "LLMJacking" follow-on and the fact that this is LiteLLM's second supply chain incident this year, both true but they crowd the ending. Handle note: @SocketSecurity is verified (their post on the compromise). LiteLLM's and BerriAI's X handles could NOT be verified, so they are not tagged.
