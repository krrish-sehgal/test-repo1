what the story is: the AI vulnerability research firm Xint published "compaction smuggling" on Mon 26 Oct 2026. OpenAI's API returns reasoning traces encrypted, so callers see the answer but not the chain of thought. An August 2026 mitigation blocked replaying captured reasoning into a *different* model. The new technique works in three API calls: capture the encrypted reasoning from a task, then call the compaction endpoint with the original task stripped out and an instruction to preserve the reasoning — and because compaction runs on the same model that produced it, the cross-model block never triggers. The instruction was formatted as JSON with every space replaced by a tab, which slipped past OpenAI's usage-policy classifiers. The output was then passed to other models, which rendered it as plaintext. OpenAI confirmed the issue, deployed a fix on 23 Oct and paid a $6,000 bounty.

**Deliberately handled:** the post says "openai's newer models" rather than naming a version — outlets split between GPT-5.6 and gpt-5.6-sol, and the exact model is not load-bearing for the story. The $6,000 is the ending because it is the most surprising verified number in the story, and it is stated flat with no editorialising, which is what makes it land. "the closest thing there is to a recipe for how the model works" is a plain-language gloss on why reasoning traces are considered proprietary, not a claim that the traces alone reproduce a model.

**Left out, deliberately:** Xint's distillation warning — that exposed reasoning could let competitors train cheaper models — is a stated risk, not a demonstrated case, so it is not in the post. SecurityWeek paired this story with a CISA warning about extensive extraction from US frontier models by Chinese companies; **no source establishes that anyone used compaction smuggling to do that**, and including it would have implied a link that does not exist, so the whole thread is omitted.
