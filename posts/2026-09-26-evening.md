@Samsung

What the story is: from 22 Sept, owners in South Korea reported that Samsung Bespoke AI 4-Door
refrigerators (2024 models onward) stopped cooling after an over-the-air firmware update delivered
through the SmartThings app. Affected units showed their LED lights off and a panel message reading
"Checking SmartThings app during update" and went no further. It landed days before Chuseok, Korea's
three-day mid-autumn harvest festival, when households have already stocked up to cook; one owner
reported discarding 20kg of food. Samsung halted distribution of the firmware, said the file was an
internal testing build mistakenly released, promised an emergency fix and free repairs, and said
food-spoilage compensation is "under review" rather than confirmed. Some customers reported repair
appointments slipping into October. Verified against Fortune, Korea JoongAng Daily, Tom's Hardware,
TechSpot, Cybernews, Seoul Economic Daily, SamMobile, Notebookcheck and Android Headlines, which
agree on the SmartThings delivery, the Bespoke AI 4-Door line, the panel message, the 20kg figure,
the "internal test file" explanation and compensation being under review. Outlets differ on exactly
how many units were hit, so the post gives no number.

@Microsoft @Surface @Qualcomm

What the story is: reported 25 Sept, Microsoft has quietly stopped using the "Copilot+ PC" brand it
launched in May 2024. The new 12-inch Surface Pro and 13-inch Surface Laptop shown at Qualcomm's
Snapdragon Summit both meet the original hardware bar (an NPU rated at 40+ TOPS, 16GB RAM, 8 logical
processors) but carry no Copilot+ badge. Surface CVP Brett Ostrum confirmed on the record: "these
are not called Copilot+ PCs. They do meet all the requirements of our previous bar for what Copilot+
devices are." Qualcomm's SVP of compute and gaming Kedar Kondap told Windows Central the badge is
unlikely to be used going forward, saying the goal is still on-device AI "probably without just
using Copilot+ PC terminology now." Windows Central's Zac Bowden traces the brand damage to the 2024
Recall launch: Recall captured periodic screenshots so users could search their history, security
researchers (notably Kevin Beaumont) showed the stored data was trivially readable, Microsoft
delayed it in June 2024, and first-generation Copilot+ machines shipped without their defining
feature. Verified against Windows Central, PCWorld, 9to5Google, The Verge (via aggregators),
TechSpot-adjacent trade press, VideoCardz and How-To Geek, which agree on the Ostrum quote, the
Kondap quote, the unbadged Surfaces and the Recall backstory. No outlet frames this as a formal
announcement, and the post says so.
