@MIT

What the story is: MIT engineers published new work (21–22 Sept coverage) on an AI-based controller
for their insect-scale flapping-wing aerial microrobot — a deep-learned robust tube model predictive
controller, arXiv 2508.03043. It raises the robot's speed by about 450% and acceleration by about
250% over the team's own previous best results, and lets it perform aerobatics including ten
somersaults in eleven seconds, while never straying more than 4–5 cm from its planned trajectory.
The robot is roughly the size of a microcassette, lighter than a paperclip, and uses soft artificial
muscles to drive its flapping wings. The December 2025 predecessor work is the "flies as fast as a
bumblebee" result. Intended application is searching earthquake rubble and spaces conventional drones
cannot enter. Verified against ScienceDaily, SciTechDaily, Interesting Engineering, The Brighter Side
of News and the arXiv preprint, plus MIT's own AeroAstro/EECS/Schwarzman pages for the December
result.
