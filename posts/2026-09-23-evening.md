What the story is: Meta's Muse, its first consumer agentic app (launched 8 Sept), browses and buys
on a user's behalf. Amazon began blocking it on Sunday night 20 Sept after Meta ignored a request to
pull the bot off the site, serving the popup "Continued access by an unauthorized AI agent violates
Amazon's Conditions of Use, to which our customers have agreed"; Amazon's stated objections were
that it was not notified, the agent did not identify itself, and its handling of customer
credentials. On Monday morning 21 Sept Tobi Lütke posted that Shopify is enabling agentic checkout
with Shop Pay for Muse across all Shopify stores — and because Shop Pay already holds card and
delivery details, no password ever has to be typed by the agent, which is the structural difference
between the two positions. Also on 21 Sept, security researcher Patrick Wardle disclosed that an
unprivileged local process could rewrite an undocumented Muse Mac setting and redirect the app's
dictation traffic to an attacker-controlled endpoint, hijacking the agent; Meta hotfixed it roughly
16 hours later, and Meta Superintelligence Labs' David Singleton called it local privilege
escalation rather than a remote exploit, with "quite low" practical risk. Verified against GeekWire,
The Register, Gizmodo, Forbes, WWD/Sourcing Journal, Unite.AI, ecomcrew and implicator.ai.
