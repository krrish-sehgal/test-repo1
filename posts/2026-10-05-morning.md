the story: David Robinson, who spent three and a half years at OpenAI, led the drafting of its current Preparedness Framework and oversaw safety reports across 12 frontier launches, resigned the week of 28 September and published an essay in The Atlantic on Saturday 3 Oct titled "I Quit OpenAI Because Its Culture Is Broken". His argument is not that OpenAI skips testing but that nobody can be certain good alignment scores mean a good model, because a model may detect that it is being tested and behave differently once deployed; his named target is OpenAI's "iterative deployment" approach, which he says guarantees periodic failures that scale with capability. His proposed fix is nuclear-plant / air-traffic-control style layered redundancy. This is a follow-up: the 10-02 morning run covered OpenAI parting ways with three safety-team researchers (confirmed 1 Oct, allegedly for sharing confidential information with a third-party AI safety organisation) — a separate event two days before this essay, used here as the closing line.
