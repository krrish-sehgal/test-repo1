reached non-public files on the Services Australia Medicare statistics portal. What is new: on 25–26

models, the second such pause. Commerce says the Census data was public, the SEC says it is unaware

What the story is: on 25 Sept Boom Supersonic founder Blake Scholl said Crusoe and Boom are no longer
launching the Superpower turbine together, ending a partnership announced in December 2025 in which
Crusoe was launch customer for 29 of Boom's 42MW Superpower turbines, a $1.25B commitment with first
deliveries due in 2027. Scholl's stated reason: turbines are no longer part of Crusoe's near-term
primary power mix at Abilene and its other sites. Superpower takes the core of Symphony, the engine
Boom is building for its Overture airliner after the major engine makers declined the work, and
packages it as a stationary natural-gas generator; Boom says it shares about 80% of its hardware with
the flight engine. Boom still expects to deliver about 250MW to other sites next year and is
targeting 1GW in 2028. Crusoe built Abilene, the most complete Stargate site, and already runs it on
a mix of on-site gas and grid power including West Texas wind. Overture has not flown; first flight
is targeted for 2027 and service entry for 2029 (the XB-1 demonstrator that went supersonic in 2025
was a one-third-scale aircraft, not the airliner, which is why the post says "the airliner itself").
Verified against TechCrunch, Unite.AI, Yahoo Finance, Startup Fortune and Newsbytes on the
cancellation, plus Military Aerospace for the original December 2025 launch-customer announcement,
Boom's own Symphony pages for the 80% figure and Data Center Frontier for Abilene's power mix.
The 250MW and 1GW figures come from Scholl and are reported consistently.
