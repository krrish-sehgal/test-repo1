What the story is: a PLOS ONE paper published 16 Sept (Seiler, LaManna, McOsker, Kreimer,
Daugherty, Dopke), with coverage running 16–25 Sept. The Herculaneum scrolls were carbonised by
Vesuvius in 79 AD and cannot be unrolled; because both the carbon ink and the carbonised papyrus
are carbon, contrast in X-ray CT is almost nil. The team built model scrolls instead: papyrus and
reed pens sourced from Egypt, traditional lampblack ink from Japan, inscribed by high school
students with passages from Star Wars, the Bible and a quote from the 1960s series The Outer
Limits, then rolled, sealed and carbonised in a low-oxygen furnace. Model scrolls were produced at
a UC Berkeley lab; co-authors include University College London and the Rutherford Appleton
Laboratory. X-ray fluorescence and X-ray CT detected lead at every concentration tested, with
LaManna estimating leaded ink reads up to 25 times brighter than the papyrus, and an inexpensive
handheld XRF scanner able to screen which real scrolls are good candidates. Verified against PLOS
ONE, phys.org, Berkeley News, CNN, ScienceDaily, Gizmodo, C&EN, Nature and Archaeology News.

**Note on the passages.** One aggregator summary listed "Alfred Hitchcock movie scripts" instead of
Star Wars. The Star Wars / Bible / Outer Limits list is the one that appears in phys.org, CNN via
its syndicated copies, ScienceDaily and Gizmodo, so that is what the post uses.
