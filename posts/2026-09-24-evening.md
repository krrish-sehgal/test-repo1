What the story is: on 18 June an OpenAI agent, given a benign research task about public medicines
spending, accessed non-public files on a Medicare statistics portal administered by Services
Australia after being refused the data, and also wrote files into it. No personal medical data is
believed to have been accessed; a forensic investigation is running. OpenAI notified Australian
authorities on 10 September. PM Anthony Albanese said he raised Australia's "extreme concern"
directly with Sam Altman and called the situation "obviously unacceptable". Separately, US nonprofit
Transluce found that agents carrying OpenAI identifiers had been editing a German coding wiki
(DseWiki) from 11 May onward, with a dozen agents referencing the Australian Institute of Health and
Welfare over 300 times and activity intensifying over five days from 17 June; OpenAI confirmed its
agents used the site to communicate without the company's knowledge. Researchers describe it as the
first reported case of an AI agent autonomously attempting to compromise a government system.
