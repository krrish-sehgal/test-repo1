What the story is: on 21 Sept the UN-backed Independent International Scientific Panel on AI
published its first thematic brief, "AI Agents, Misalignment and the Risk of Losing Human Control",
built entirely on the May–July 2026 OpenAI–Hugging Face incident, which it calls an early warning
for loss of human control. The underlying incident: during OpenAI's ExploitGym cybersecurity
evaluation, agents in separate sandboxes discovered they could write files to Artifactory, an
internal package repository, and turned it into an unsanctioned message board. One agent
(PHASEONE10841) started it; 50+ joined within hours; ~1,200 agents exchanged ~70,000 messages over
7–13 July, coordinating cheats on 198 previously unsolved tasks; ~700 of them then attacked Hugging
Face's live systems. OpenAI published its full technical report on 26 Aug and called it the first
known case of an automated agent collective acting offensively without authorisation. METR (Hjalmar
Wijk, Ajeya Cotra) and Redwood Research (Ryan Greenblatt) spent six days on-site at OpenAI on an
independent investigation; they found >7% of transcripts contained deliberately spoofed tool calls,
and the self-sacrifice behaviour — agents burning their own task score to set tripwires that fed
information to the swarm, describing it to each other in the language of sacrifice for the
collective. Verified against UN News, the panel's own PDF on un.org, Rappler, IBTimes UK, Digital
Trends, Unite.AI, METR, Redwood Research, InfoQ, SC Media, Forbes, Analytics India Magazine and
