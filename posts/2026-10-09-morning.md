what the story is: on 8 Oct 2026 the US Department of Labor suspended eight companies from the PERM
labour-certification programme, the step an employer must clear before sponsoring a worker for permanent
residency. The eight: Microsoft, Adobe, Cognizant, Capgemini, Infosys, TCS, Wipro and HCLTech. The
department will neither accept new PERM applications nor process pending ones involving those companies.

under the US Anti-Fraud Task Force; Sonderling's stated reason is multiple active federal investigations,
and Labor Inspector General Anthony D'Esposito said subpoenas have been served. Vance's remarks focused on
Microsoft, noting it laid off thousands of workers in the past year while still applying for H-1Bs and
subsequent green cards; Sonderling's line is that "there has been no company in the United States,
unfortunately, that has abused this system more than Microsoft". **The closing beat is the whole reason to

what the story is: on 6 Oct 2026 OpenAI published github.com/openai/math under Apache-2.0: 722 manuscripts
organised into 372 result families, each claiming to resolve or substantially advance an open mathematical
question across roughly 20 subfields, all produced by an unreleased internal frontier model. OpenAI posed
the model about 4,000 open research problems and published what cleared its own significance bar, so 722 is
a count of manuscripts retained, not problems solved. On 7 Oct, one day later, OpenAI withdrew three
manuscripts over a sign error: the flawed paper is "Algebraicity of Weil classes on split abelian
eightfolds", which used +1 where its own conventions required −1, invalidating its argument and the
construction two dependent papers relied on ("Algebraicity of Kuga–Satake Correspondences for K3 Surfaces"
and "The rational Hodge conjecture for products of K3 surfaces"). OpenAI also revised 14 papers and updated
13 citations, taking the catalogue from 722 to 719. As of 7 Oct, 300 of 719 top-line results (~42%) had
machine-checked Lean proofs, and the repo's README warns that results without a Lean version may contain
errors. MIT's Andrew Sutherland: "Until and unless they release the model and people can replicate their
results, I think you should treat any claims about one-shotting problems with a single agent as
unverified... We should ask for receipts." OpenAI published average compute figures and ten reasoning
summaries but no prompts, which falls short of what an Institute for Advanced Study advisory group
recommended on 29 Sept. **The closing line is a company spokesperson's own statement**, which is why it
