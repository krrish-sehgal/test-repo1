what the story is: Dr Magnus Lynch, a consultant dermatologist at Guy's Hospital, published a head-to-head test on 25 Oct 2026 (it hit the HN front page the same day at 449 points). 200 histopathology-confirmed lesions from the public MILK10k dataset, each shown as a clinical photo plus a dermoscopic image, six diagnoses to choose from. First-choice accuracy: fine-tuned Inception v3 125/200 (62%), GPT-6 Astra 84 (42%), Claude Fable 5.1 85 (42%). Melanoma first-choice: CNN 13 of 30, GPT-6 Astra 8, Claude 11. Allowing a second guess: CNN 173/200 vs 134 and 138. Asked from a patient's perspective, both LLMs advised seeing a doctor for nearly every lesion including nearly all the biopsy-proven melanomas. The ending is the genuinely uncomfortable number and the reason the post does not read as "old model good, new models bad": at its preset threshold the CNN discharged 18 of 30 melanomas, and only when Lynch matched its discharge rate to the LLMs' did it hold back all but 1 or 2. Lynch's own caveats, which the post signals with "a small test" rather than burying: only 30 melanomas so wide confidence intervals, each LLM case run once, the MILK10k images are public so the LLMs may have seen them in training, and the CNN is a fine-tuned baseline not a state-of-the-art model. He published test cases, prompts, response logs and scoring scripts. This rests on one primary source plus secondary write-ups and has not been independently replicated, which is why the post is framed as one doctor's test and the reply says so too.
