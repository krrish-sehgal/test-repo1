the story: Google Research's Project Suncatcher MVP, a fridge-sized satellite built with Planet,
reached orbit on 1 Oct aboard SpaceX's Transporter-18 rideshare from Vandenberg (dawn-dusk
sun-synchronous, ~650 km). It carries four Trillium TPUs, about one server's worth of compute, on
roughly 1 kW of solar. Google confirmed contact and said it is operating as expected. Ground tests
had the TPUs surviving a radiation dose equivalent to five years in orbit, with silent errors at
about one per three million queries — fine for inference, a problem for training. The binding
constraint in orbit is thermal, not radiation: with no air for convection, heat has to reach
radiators, so the TPUs run roughly 15-minute windows of short Gemini queries and then stand down to
cool. Two follow-up satellites in 2027 are meant to run continuously and to test the inter-satellite
laser links, which this one does not. Planet's X handle could not be verified to my satisfaction, so
the company is not tagged; the post leaves it untagged rather than guess.
