## The one search that succeeded, and why nothing was built on it

Query: "OpenAI Anthropic news this week". It returned a synthesis citing mostly
**aiweekly.co** (an LLM-generated news aggregator) plus one real CNBC URL. Claims included:

- Anthropic released "Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1"
- Anthropic reassigned 150 engineers and froze RL for a month after Claude sandbox escapes
- OpenAI released "GPT-6 Astra"; paused two weeks of RL training after a Hugging Face breach
- Evan Hubinger (a real, named Anthropic employee) quoted giving a >10%/decade estimate that
  "AI could kill all humans"
- CNBC, 2026-09-06: "'Model fatigue' sets in as AI labs race to roll out new versions"

**None of it was posted.** Reasons, in order of weight:

1. **No second source.** The routine requires two independent outlets per fact. This is one
   search response, and its underlying citation is largely a single aggregator. Every
   follow-up query attempting to corroborate any of these items failed with `unavailable`,
   and the aggregator pages themselves are egress-blocked.
2. **These are high-stakes claims about real people and companies.** A dramatic quote
   attributed to a named individual, a specific operational claim about a company's staffing,
   and a security breach at a named company. Publishing any of those in the user's own voice,
   under their name, on the strength of one unverifiable LLM-generated aggregator is how you
   put a false and potentially defamatory claim on a real person's timeline. Not worth it.
3. **Internal inconsistency in the source.** The aggregator mixed in an obviously stale item
   ("Gemini v GPT-3 & 4") alongside the current-sounding ones, which is the signature of
   generated filler rather than reporting.
4. **The one item from a real outlet doesn't clear the bar anyway.** The CNBC "model fatigue"
   piece is 4 days old (outside the 24–48h window) and its thesis — labs ship models fast — is
   generic. It fails the "smart person stops scrolling" test even if verified.

Sending nothing beats sending three posts built on a single aggregator's word.

## Fix for future runs

Unchanged from the previous run, and now confirmed across two fires:

- **Widen the environment's network egress policy** to allow the news domains this routine
  needs (news.ycombinator.com, techcrunch.com, reuters.com, theverge.com,
  economictimes.indiatimes.com, and RSS endpoints). The policy is chosen when the environment
  is created — see https://code.claude.com/docs/en/claude-code-on-the-web. This is the single
  highest-value fix: with fetches working, one lucky WebSearch becomes verifiable.
- **Keep retrying WebSearch.** It is flaky, not dead. A run that issues many queries will land
  some. Future runs should budget for a low hit rate rather than standing down after 3–5 misses.
