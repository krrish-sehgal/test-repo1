what the story is: CVE-2026-80097 in Microsoft Authenticator for Android, disclosed publicly by the security firm Enclave on 23 Oct 2026 and widely covered on 27 Oct. Authenticator shipped an exported Android activity (com.microsoft.identity.client.AccountChooserActivity) that any app on the device could launch, and the broker service trusted the caller's self-declared identity rather than verifying it, so a zero-permission app could impersonate a trusted Microsoft app such as Teams and request tokens. In Enclave's tests the tokens gave full access to the account's mail, files and calendar, and impersonating Authenticator itself reportedly also allowed creating or deleting passkeys. Microsoft assigned the CVE on 8 Sept, rated it 8.6 (Important), says it has not been exploited, and fixed it in Authenticator 6.2606.4246. The post keeps the one real disagreement in the reporting: Enclave says its test needed no further user interaction, Microsoft's advisory says exploitation requires the user to complete an authentication flow. The passkey detail is left out as it appears in fewer outlets. Research credited to Ofek Levin through a fellowship at Enclave; Enclave is not @tagged because I could not verify its handle.
